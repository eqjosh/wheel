EMOTIONAL WISDOM WHEEL - STORY EXAMPLES DRAFT
=============================================
Each feeling has a brief real-life scenario illustrating when someone might experience it.

================================================================================
JOY
================================================================================

OPTIMISTIC
----------
Maria had been job hunting for three months with no luck. On the way to yet another interview, she caught herself smiling on the subway. "I'm going to nail this one -- I've done the hard work," she thought. Instead of rehearsing what could go wrong, she found herself imagining her first day at the new office. The rational part of her brain warned against getting her hopes up, but something in her gut said this time she was really ready.

CONFIDENT
---------
After months of preparation, James stood backstage waiting to give his first TEDx talk. His hands were steady, his breathing calm. He'd practiced this speech fifty times, knew every transition, every pause. When they called his name, he walked to center stage not with arrogance, but with quiet certainty. He had put in the work. He was ready.

JOYFUL
------
The letter arrived on a Tuesday. Accepted. Full scholarship. Elena read it three times before it sank in. Then she was laughing, crying, spinning around her tiny apartment. She called her mother, her best friend, her old teacher who'd written her recommendation. Pure, unfiltered happiness—the kind that makes your face hurt from smiling.

LOVING
------
David watched his daughter take her first wobbly steps across the living room. When she fell, she looked up at him with complete trust, waiting for his reaction before deciding whether to cry. He scooped her up, kissed her forehead, and felt his heart expand in a way he couldn't have imagined before becoming a father. This tiny person had utterly transformed him.

================================================================================
TRUST
================================================================================

GRATEFUL
--------
After the surgery, lying in the recovery room, Tom found himself overwhelmed—not by pain, but by appreciation. The nurse who'd held his hand before anesthesia. His sister who'd taken time off work to be there. The surgeon whose steady hands had given him more years with his family. He'd never felt so aware of how much he depended on others, or so thankful for it.

PEACEFUL
--------
Sunday morning. Rain against the windows. No alarms, no obligations, nowhere to be. Sarah curled up with her coffee and a book she'd been meaning to read for months. The apartment was quiet except for her cat purring on the cushion beside her. For the first time in weeks, her mind wasn't racing through to-do lists. She was simply... here.

ACCEPTED
--------
Coming out to his parents had been terrifying. Miguel had prepared for arguments, disappointment, maybe even rejection. Instead, his mother had pulled him into a hug and whispered, "I know, mijo. I've known for a while." His father had simply said, "You're still you. Nothing changes that." Years of fear dissolved in that moment of unconditional acceptance.

HOPEFUL
-------
The cancer was in remission. Dr. Chen had used careful language—"cautiously optimistic," "good indicators"—but walking out of the oncology center, Rachel felt something she hadn't allowed herself to feel in eighteen months: hope. Not certainty, not denial, but genuine hope. The future, which had seemed so closed off, suddenly had doors again.

================================================================================
FEAR
================================================================================

NERVOUS
-------
First day at the new school. Aisha stood outside the classroom door, hearing laughter and conversation inside. What if no one talked to her? What if she couldn't find the cafeteria? What if she said something stupid? Her stomach churned. She took a breath, reminded herself she'd survived new situations before, and pushed open the door.

SCARED
------
The smoke alarm went off at 2 AM. Heart pounding, Kevin grabbed his phone and ran to check the kitchen. Nothing. The hallway. Nothing. Then he smelled it—smoke seeping under his apartment door from the hallway. This was real. His hands shook as he called 911, grabbed his wallet, and headed for the fire escape. Everything felt suddenly, terrifyingly immediate.

ANXIOUS
-------
The presentation wasn't for another week, but Lisa couldn't stop thinking about it. What if the projector failed? What if the CEO asked a question she couldn't answer? What if her voice shook? She lay awake at 3 AM, her mind spinning through worst-case scenarios, each worry branching into three more. She knew this spiral wasn't helpful, but couldn't seem to stop it.

INSECURE
--------
Everyone at the dinner party seemed to know each other from business school. They traded references to professors and inside jokes while Marcus smiled and nodded, feeling increasingly invisible. "What do you do?" someone finally asked him, and suddenly his job—which he'd been proud of that morning—seemed inadequate. Who was he to be here among these people?

================================================================================
SURPRISE
================================================================================

STARTLED
--------
Walking home through the quiet park, headphones in, Priya was lost in a podcast when something burst from the bushes beside her. She jumped back, heart racing, hands up defensively—only to watch a squirrel scamper across the path. She laughed shakily, feeling foolish, but her pulse took several minutes to return to normal.

CONFUSED
--------
The email made no sense. After five years of stellar reviews, Daniel was being "transitioned to a new role"—effective immediately. His boss wouldn't return calls. HR spoke in careful non-answers. He sat at his desk, reading the same sentences over and over, trying to fit the pieces together. What had happened? What had he missed?

AMAZED
------
Standing at the rim of the Grand Canyon for the first time, words failed her. Photographs hadn't prepared Jennifer for this—the impossible scale, the layers of color, the silence broken only by wind. She'd seen beautiful places before, but this was different. This made her feel small in a way that was somehow comforting, connected to something vast and ancient.

DISAPPOINTED
------------
"We've decided to go in a different direction." The words hung in the air of the gallery. Chloe had spent eight months on this commission, pouring her vision into every brushstroke. The client had seemed so enthusiastic in their meetings. Now they wanted "something more conventional." She smiled professionally, shook hands, and waited until she reached her car to let the tears fall.

================================================================================
SADNESS
================================================================================

HURT
----
"I just don't think you're leadership material." The words from his mentor—someone he'd trusted for years—landed like a punch. Robert had shared his dreams of running his own department someday, made himself vulnerable. Now that vulnerability felt like a mistake. The criticism might have been constructive, but wrapped in that dismissive phrase, it just felt like betrayal.

DEPRESSED
---------
Getting out of bed had become the hardest part. Emma would lie there, knowing she had things to do, people counting on her, and feel nothing—just a heavy emptiness that made even showering feel like climbing a mountain. Colors seemed muted. Food had no taste. She went through the motions of her life while feeling like she was watching it from far away.

LONELY
------
The apartment was full of boxes from the move. New city, new job, fresh start—everything she'd wanted. But sitting on her bare mattress that first night, phone full of contacts yet no one to call, Yuki felt the weight of her aloneness. She could hear her neighbors through the walls, living their lives, and the sounds only made the silence in her own space louder.

ASHAMED
-------
It was just a shortcut, he'd told himself. A few copied paragraphs in a paper no one would read closely anyway. But when the professor called him into her office, evidence spread across her desk, Michael couldn't meet her eyes. The disappointment in her voice was worse than anger would have been. He'd betrayed not just academic rules, but his own sense of who he was.

================================================================================
DISGUST
================================================================================

DISLIKE
-------
Every comment in the meeting, every "well, actually," every interruption—Grace felt her jaw tighten a little more. She couldn't point to anything overtly offensive about her new colleague, but something about his manner set her teeth on edge. By the meeting's end, she knew she'd need to find ways to limit their interactions if she wanted to stay professional.

AVOIDANCE
---------
The pile of mail on the counter had grown into a small mountain. Bills, probably. Maybe something from the IRS. Omar walked past it every day, made coffee inches away from it, and somehow never quite managed to open any of it. He knew this was making things worse. He also knew he wasn't going to deal with it today. Probably not tomorrow either.

AWFUL
-----
The documentary showed footage from the factory farms—animals in conditions she couldn't unsee. Natalie had known, intellectually, where her food came from. But knowing and seeing were different things. She closed her laptop, stomach churning, feeling implicated in something she'd never really let herself think about before.

DISAPPROVAL
-----------
Watching his son scroll through his phone during their grandmother's birthday speech, Richard felt his expression harden. The old woman was sharing memories of her late husband, her voice cracking with emotion, and Tyler couldn't be bothered to look up for five minutes. Richard caught his son's eye and gave a slow shake of his head. Some things shouldn't need to be explained.

================================================================================
ANGER
================================================================================

AGGRESSIVE
----------
"Say that again." Victor's voice was low, dangerous. The man at the bar had made a comment about his wife—loud enough to be heard, quiet enough to deny. Victor felt his hands curl into fists, felt the familiar heat rising in his chest. Every instinct screamed at him to make this guy regret opening his mouth. His wife's hand on his arm was the only thing holding him back.

MAD
---
Three hours. Three hours she'd been waiting for the cable company, watching the window between "12 and 4" slowly tick away. When her phone finally rang at 4:30—"We need to reschedule"—Diane felt something snap. The carefully worded complaint she'd prepared vanished, replaced by a flood of every frustration she'd swallowed for months. The customer service rep didn't deserve it, but he got it anyway.

FRUSTRATED
----------
The code should work. Alex had checked every line, compared it to the documentation, even copied examples directly. Still, the same error message, mocking him in red. He'd been at this for six hours. His coffee was cold. The solution was probably something obvious, something he'd kick himself for missing. But right now, all he wanted was to throw his laptop out the window.

CRITICAL
--------
"It's fine, I guess." Sophie watched her roommate's face fall at the lukewarm response to her new painting. She hadn't meant to be harsh, but she could see every flaw—the muddy colors, the uncertain brushwork, the derivative composition. The kindest thing would have been encouraging words. Instead, she'd let her standards speak for her, and now the moment to be supportive had passed.

================================================================================
ANTICIPATION
================================================================================

EXCITED
-------
The concert was still three weeks away, but Jasmine had already planned her outfit, made a playlist of songs to re-learn, and started counting down the days. She'd been following this band since middle school, knew every album, every interview. The fact that she'd actually gotten tickets—good tickets—still didn't feel entirely real.

EAGER
-----
When the email came through—"Your application has been approved"—Ben was already grabbing his car keys. The rescue shelter closed in an hour, and somewhere inside was the dog he'd seen online, the one with the goofy ears and the soulful eyes. Six months of researching breeds, preparing his apartment, waiting for the right moment. Today was the day.

INTERESTED
----------
"Tell me more about that." Professor Walsh leaned forward, genuinely curious. The student's thesis proposal wasn't polished yet, but there was something there—an unexpected connection between fields that rarely spoke to each other. Her mind was already racing with questions, potential research directions, colleagues who might want to collaborate. This was why she loved academia.

STRESSED
--------
The wedding was in ten days. The caterer had just canceled. Her mother-in-law was "suggesting" seating chart changes. The alterations on her dress weren't done. And now, scrolling through her phone at midnight, Hannah discovered that the forecast called for rain. She could feel the pressure building behind her eyes, the familiar tightness in her chest. Something was going to have to give.

END OF DRAFT

Notes for review:
- Each story is approximately 100-150 words
- Stories aim to be relatable, diverse, and specific
- Names and scenarios vary to represent different demographics
- Stories show the feeling in action rather than just describing it
- Some stories include the adaptive/useful aspect of the emotion
- Tone matches the intensity level of each feeling

Please review and let me know:
1. Any stories that don't resonate or feel off
2. Feelings that need different scenarios
3. Stories that are too long/short
4. Any problematic content or representations
5. Whether the tone/style works for your audience
